Residual feedback can improve a fixed time series forecaster, but compressing the residual history can discard useful observations. We propose endpoint-preserving compressed residual correction, which retains discrete cosine transform coefficients of the preceding fully observed residual block together with its final residual value. Separate online ridge regressions combine these inputs with coefficients of the current base forecast. The correction remains in the compressed output subspace and is blended using statistics from completed blocks. We evaluate 24-step forecasts on six multivariate datasets with two base architectures, and separately examine two additional building sites. Across 72 conditions combining datasets, architectures, seeds, and base-training variants, the method reduces mean squared error by an average of 16.99\% relative to the uncorrected forecast, with a median of 4528 retained auxiliary bytes. On separately trained base models, it reduces mean squared error by 16.69--20.15\% relative to learned residual adapters across the tested widths and warmup settings. Comparisons with equally sized first, middle, and mean residual summaries favor the endpoint in mean squared and mean absolute error on all 12 dataset--architecture pairs after seed averaging under both warmup settings. A larger online Fourier regressor achieves lower average error, while smaller adapters retain fewer bytes. A selected adverse interval also demonstrates that average improvement does not prevent local deterioration. The results support explicit endpoint retention as an effective input construction for compressed residual correction and characterize its accuracy--storage tradeoff. Code and numerical results are available at \url{https://github.com/keiotakmin/endpoint-preserving-residual-correction}.
